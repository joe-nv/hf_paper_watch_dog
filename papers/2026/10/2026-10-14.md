## 1. [Jeva: System One Models from First Principles](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18773)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.18773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18773)

**Organization**: National University of Singapore

**Authors**: Qi Li, Xinchao Wang

**Published**: 2026-10-13 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by LIQIIIII

**Upvotes**: 118

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/LiQiiiii/Jeva) | [Project Page](https://liqiiiii.github.io/Jeva/)

### Abstract

System one models enable direct, low-latency decision-making. Yet recent practice of predicting an answer from serialized text does not, by itself, respect the structure of a decision problem. We argue that current designs underemphasize four important structural requirements. (i) null-aware: Models should distinguish answer absence from uncertainty among candidates; (ii) permutation-invariant: Reordering alternatives should not change the decision; (iii) option-scalable: Growing candidate sets should not require redefining the output space; (iv) cacheable: Request-independent computation over recurring alternatives should be reusable across requests. We introduce Jeva, a decision-native architecture that addresses these requirements jointly. A shared encoder independently represents the request and each option, while a compact decision stack refines only the request state against immutable option memory. Symmetric aggregation yields permutation-equivariant probabilities; shared scoring accommodates variable-size candidate sets. A separate coverage estimate distinguishes answer absence from candidate competition, enabling explicit null outcomes. Request-independent option representations and layer projections support reuse across newly assembled sets. These mechanisms unify Choice, Noul, and Score outputs. Across evaluations, Jeva combines strong typed-decision performance with near-perfect null detection and no decision flips under any tested permutations. It scales to thousands of candidates and substantially reduces latency through cross-request reuse without changing decisions.

## 2. [Agent-Reflex: Teaching Language Agents to Act Reflectively](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17953)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.17953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17953)

**Organization**: NLPIR Lab @ RUC

**Authors**: Guanting Dong, Xinyu Yang, Jiajie Jin, Yuyang Hu, Xiaoshuai Song, Yifei Chen, Xiaoxi Li, Jiejun Tan, Yutao Zhu, Tat-Seng Chua, Ji-Rong Wen, Zhicheng Dou

**Published**: 2026-10-13 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by KABI

**Upvotes**: 107

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/RUC-NLPIR/Agent-Reflex)

### Abstract

Large language models have evolved from single-turn chatbots into interactive agents capable of long-horizon reasoning. In real deployments, MCP tool services can return noisy feedback and users may intervene to correct the agent, but existing methods provide limited supervision for reflection learning and failure recovery. To bridge this gap, we propose Agent-Reflex, a general training framework for incentivizing proactive reflection in agents. Specifically, Agent-Reflex comprises three stages. First, Environment Reflection Skill Discovery (RSD) injects grounded tool-feedback perturbations into environment scaling, samples a capability-diverse LLM pool, mines matched success-failure pairs, and summarizes reusable reflection meta-skills. Building on this skill library, we then apply Reflective Supervised Fine-Tuning (ReSFT) that converts each skill into supervision for self-reflection, skill recovery, and user-centric correction, strengthening complementary reflective behaviors. Finally, we propose On-Policy Reflective Policy Optimization (ORPO) to combine agentic reinforcement learning under noisy interactions with reflection-guided on-policy distillation to further elicit proactive reflection. Built on Qwen3-8B, Agent-Reflex reaches 58.6 overall on five long-horizon tool-use benchmarks and 29.1 on AgentNoiseBench, surpassing strong environment-scaling and reflection baselines. Experiments on six challenging agent benchmark suites demonstrate the effectiveness of Agent-Reflex. Further analyses show that Agent-Reflex improves agents' robustness to noise while maintaining stable training dynamics, supporting scalable agent training. Our code is available at https://github.com/RUC-NLPIR/Agent-Reflex.

## 3. [Urbex: Agentic Spatial Grounding for City-Scale 3D Scenes](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18356)

**Organization**: alibaba

**Upvotes**: 101

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/AMAP-ML/Urbex) | [Project Page](https://www.wangfaye.cn/paper/urbex)

### Abstract

City-scale 3D grounding is commonly formulated as candidate ranking, which requires precomputed instance proposals and becomes inefficient in large outdoor scenes. We propose Urbex, an agentic vision-language framework that instead casts grounding as active spatial search. Urbex represents a city-scale 3D scene as an interactive multi-view environment, allowing a VLM agent to query landmarks, zoom into local regions, render oblique views, and commit a grounded bounding box. We optimize this tool-use policy with reinforcement learning on CityRefer, using smooth localization rewards and lightweight shaping rewards for efficient exploration. Experiments show that Urbex achieves the best Hit@1 and IoU on CityRefer, supports candidate-free grounding without ground-truth boxes, and is substantially faster than the strongest city-scale ranking baseline. Zero-shot results on STPLS3D-Refer further indicate improved cross-scene robustness.

## 4. [VideoJev: System One Model for Video Understanding and Interaction](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18615)

**Organization**: Multimedia Computing Group-Nanjing University

**Authors**: Yuhan Zhu, Xiangyu Zeng, Qingyi Si, Chenxu Yang, Yifei Li, Luozheng Qin, Junhao Zhou, Dingyu Yao, Jun Zhang, Chuanyu Qin, Zhiqiu Zhang, Yuandong Yang, Yuqi Xu, Tianyu Zhang, Hongcheng Guo, Jiaqi Wang, Nan Duan, Limin Wang

**Published**: 2026-10-13 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by Yuhan Zhu

**Upvotes**: 85

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/VideoJev) | [Project Page](https://mcg-nju.github.io/VideoJev/)

### Abstract

Visual agents must turn video observations into timely decisions to track progress and choose what to do next. Building on the emerging System One paradigm, we introduce VideoJev, a family of models that cast video understanding and interaction as structured decision tasks. We adapt pretrained multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by replacing autoregressive answer decoding with a shared head that scores temporal intervals and textual options against video evidence. These scores yield event spans or answer choices, unifying temporal grounding and multiple-choice video question answering. This combines the semantic flexibility of MLLMs with efficient decisions that agents can use directly. As an interaction unfolds, new goals and intermediate results prompt further queries about the same visual history. VideoJev therefore reuses visual computation through Cached inference for queries as they arrive and Joint inference for multiple queries in a single forward pass. Across three temporal grounding benchmarks, VideoJev-4B and VideoJev-35B-A3B improve mean mIoU over their backbones by 6.7 and 10.8 points, respectively, with measured inference speedups of 2.8-3.1times. On four video QA benchmarks, both achieve performance comparable to their backbones in Thinking mode, with measured inference speedups exceeding 100times. These results offer a unified, reusable decision interface for composing structured visual judgments into complex, open-ended workflows.

## 5. [TPPO: Tracking-Value PPO For Language Model Reasoning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16794)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.16794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16794)

**Authors**: Futing Wang, Chenxi Li, Jianhao Yan, Yun Luo, Zhi Wang, Yizhuo Li, Runzhe Zhan, Yu Cheng, Tao Lin

**Published**: 2026-10-12 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by wang

**Upvotes**: 63

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/LINs-lab/TPPO)

### Abstract

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is widely used in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for language-model reasoning. While value evolution along trajectory characterizes relative token-level credit, yet standard critics often fail to track it under sparse terminal rewards. To model this evolution, we analyze the relation between successive prefix values and show that they admit a Bayesian decomposition with additive updates in log-odds space. We use this additive structure to construct Tracking-Value PPO (\method), a cumulative value parameterization that sums position-wise scalar outputs into value logits. These outputs are learned through terminal-outcome supervision on the reconstructed values. \method improves aggregate mathematical reasoning scores over PPO by 7.0 and 8.4 points on Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base, respectively, while improving out-of-distribution generalization. Gains extend to Qwen3-4B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen3.5-9B and to countdown, and scientific coding tasks. Further analysis shows more accurate tracking of value evolution and more reliable advantage estimates. Our code is publicly available in https://github.com/LINs-lab/TPPO.

## 6. [Harness^2: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14224)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224)

**Organization**: Google

**Authors**: Ruiyao Xu, Yanfei Chen, Zhongying CuiZhu, Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, Yifei Ming, Han Yu, Rujun Han, Chen-Yu Lee, Tomas Pfister

**Published**: 2026-10-09 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by Ruiyao Xu

**Upvotes**: 62

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/google-research/harness2) | [Project Page](https://harness2.github.io/)

### Abstract

LLM agents' capabilities depend not only on their underlying models but also on their execution harnesses. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) offers a promising approach to automatically evolve these harnesses. However, deploying RSI in open-world settings still faces two critical challenges. First, open-world tasks are typically unseen during the harness design phase and novel at test time, leaving no ground-truth verifiers to reliably guide online harness edits. Second, the RSI process remains highly task-specific and fails to generalize to novel scenarios. We introduce Harness^2, a plug-and-play, recursive agent harnessing framework that addresses these challenges through two connected levels of recursion. Task-level recursion refines the current task's harness using a contrastive Propose-Probe-Compose step that reliably guides edits without ground-truth verifiers, and can be scaled further via parallel or sequential recursion. Harnessing-level recursion then extracts experience from these edits to update a continual improvement harness, enabling the agent to better adapt to future tasks. Across three professional benchmarks and four model-harness configurations, a single step consistently improves GLM-5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash over their base harnesses, yielding gains of up to 11.5 points on WorkBuddy-Bench, 4.5 points on JobBench, and 11.8 percentage points on LAB, with further gains as the task-level recursion budget increases.

### Abstract

Large language models are increasingly deployed in applications that act on users' long-term behavior histories, from personal assistants to recommendation and advertising systems. These applications demand the user dimension of LLM intelligence: inferring a person's preferences, intent, and likely future actions from weeks to years of behavior data. Yet current models are trained on static text or synthetic data, neither of which captures how user behavior evolves over time. Real-world behavior records, already available at scale on content platforms and in personal-assistant agents, offer a natural supervisory signal. ### Abstract

Generating information-rich visual artifacts such as slides, posters, and dashboards as code is appealing because the outputs are structured, editable, and verifiable. Image generators excel at visual design but can misrender requested content, making their outputs unreliable targets for direct reproduction. ## 9. [X-Ragent: Tool-Augmented Reinforcement Learning for Evidence-Grounded Chest X-Ray Reasoning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16811)

### Abstract

Chest X-ray (CXR) interpretation often requires integrating visual findings with task-specific evidence rather than relying on direct visual recognition alone. While tool-augmented medical agents can supply such evidence via external modules, they often lack explicit mechanisms for learning when auxiliary evidence is needed and for preventing inapplicable quantitative evidence from entering diagnostic reasoning. To address this, we propose X-Ragent, a post-training framework for tool-augmented reinforcement learning that decouples learned auxiliary-evidence seeking from validity-gated quantitative measurement acquisition for evidence-grounded CXR reasoning. ## 10. [DoSA: Dots Sparse Attention](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13990)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.13990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13990)

**Organization**: dots studio

**Authors**: Fanxu Meng, Taosong Fang

**Published**: 2026-09-26 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by mengfanxu

**Upvotes**: 60

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/llm-sys/flash-indexer)

### Abstract

Sparse attention visits few keys, yet finding them can still require scoring every key with every indexer head. We introduce DoSA (Dots Sparse Attention), a lossless method for making this computation conditional while preserving exact token selection. Its central observation is that Top-K selection requires sufficient evidence to reject a candidate, not necessarily its full score. A seed set supplies a lower bound on the winning threshold. Nonnegative head projections and joint residual bounds upper-bound positive contributions, while activation-informed aggregation bounds the magnitude subtracted by negative heads. The certificate directs full scoring toward candidates that remain eligible; exact-score filtering further reduces writeback and selection work. We establish the certificate and its exact-selection conditions, and analyze how geometry and selection margins govern its effectiveness. Our GPU implementation accelerates the complete Indexer by up to 2.74 times over SGLang and 1.69 times over LiteTopK across English, Chinese, and code workloads. End-to-end evaluation on GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves up to 1.94 times and 1.50 times time-to-first-token speedups over the two baselines, respectively.

## 12. [Scoring Higher, Answering Worse: Mitigating Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based RL via Protocol-Level Rubrics](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38847)

**ArXiv ID**: [2609.38847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38847)

**Organization**: ByteDance

**Authors**: Maoqi Liu, Junwei He, Bowen Zhang, Feiran Li, Wentao Ma, Rongyi Lin, Shuhan Zhong, Quan Fang

**Published**: 2026-09-30 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by quinn

**Upvotes**: 58

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Estrellajer/ProRubric)

### Abstract

Rubric-based reinforcement learning (Rubric-RL) trains language models where no verifier exists. A judge checks each criterion of a rubric, and the verdicts are aggregated into a reward, most often by a weighted sum. We show that this additive aggregation is the weak point. Under a sum, criteria compensate for one another: a policy that misses the one decision that matters can buy the points back with advice nobody asked for. On clinical consultation, such a policy scores higher and answers worse. Rubric coverage rises while appropriateness on held-out physician criteria falls below the untrained model. The medical criteria are not to blame. Grouped so that they must hold together, the same criteria, unchanged to the word, recover a third of the loss; shorter answers recover almost none. We therefore propose Protocol-level Rubrics (ProRubric), which keeps what the criteria ask for and changes how they are aggregated. It groups a checklist into a few protocol-level dimensions. A dimension counts only when all of its criteria hold and its failure clause does not fire. The grouping is done once, offline, and leaves the optimizer unchanged. ProRubric raises appropriateness by 10.8 points without losing coverage and has the best seven-benchmark average at both scales. Reward validity is set not only by what a rubric verifies, but by how it aggregates. Code is available at https://github.com/Estrellajer/ProRubric

## 14. [Multics: Generative Multi-Player World Model with Hierarchical Communication](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17201)

## 16. [UltraBERT: Beyond the Limit of BERT Pre-Training](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15028)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.15028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15028)

**Authors**: Jungwoo Park, Taewhoo Lee, Jaewoo Kang

**Published**: 2026-10-10 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-14 by Jungwoo Park

**Upvotes**: 42

### Abstract

Recent bidirectional encoders have been modernized with innovations from causal large language models (LLMs). However, the pretraining recipe itself remains inherited from small-scale conventions, leaving encoders underoptimized, with no single model performing competitively across both classification and retrieval tasks. In this work, we investigate how far encoder performance can be pushed by revisiting core design choices in training, revealing three key findings: (1) Encoders saturate on downstream tasks beyond a few trillion tokens, indicating that additional compute past this point does not help. (2) Across model sizes, lower masking ratios benefit classification while higher ones benefit retrieval, contradicting prior work suggesting larger ratios for larger models. (3) Distillation with pruning improves classification at low masking ratios but degrades retrieval at high ones. Guided by these findings, we present UltraBERT, a single encoder that assigns separate LM heads to low and high masking ratios within a batch and restricts the distillation objective to the low ratio. To our knowledge, UltraBERT is the first encoder to achieve state of the art on both classification and retrieval tasks, reaching 91.0 on GLUE and 47.7 on BEIR. Together, these results show how far encoder performance can be pushed and provide a principled basis for compute allocation, masking ratio design, and knowledge transfer in bidirectional models.

### Abstract

Multilingual translation requires a model to preserve meaning while producing text in a requested language. We present HexaBee-4B, a translator adapted from Gemma 3 4B IT through three stages: Wikipedia continued pretraining, joint multilingual answering and answer-text translation, and bidirectional translation specialization. The intermediate stage connects two tasks through shared multilingual answers: answering a question in the requested language and translating an answer into that language. It comprises 19.46 million answering records, including 5.76 million cross-language question-answer pairs, and 10.70 million answer-text translations. The final stage uses 658,274 forward and reverse synthetic translation examples. On 36,572 synthetic test examples spanning all 30 directions among Arabic, Chinese, English, Hebrew, Persian, and Spanish, HexaBee-4B achieves direction-macro chrF++ of 73.75, compared with 57.11 for Gemma 3 4B IT and 66.83 for Gemma 3 12B IT. BLEU, TER, COMET, and a matched model-judged subset support the same overall ranking. These results demonstrate improved translation across the six-language set, with chrF++ gains in every evaluated direction over both baselines.
