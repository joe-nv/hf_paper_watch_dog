## 1. [Any Time, Any Space: A World-State-Centric Framework for Agentic Video Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20571)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.20571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20571)

**Organization**: Zhejiang University

**Authors**: Weijie Wang, Haomin Zhang, Ye Li, Akide Liu, Zeyu Zhang, Haoyu Zhao, Yefei He, Donny Y. Chen, Feng Chen, Bohan Zhuang

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Weijie Wang

**Upvotes**: 206

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/ziplab/ATAS) | [Project Page](https://lhmd.top/atas/)

### Abstract

Long-form agentic video generation must turn a screenplay into shots that remain spatially and temporally consistent across nonconsecutive requests. Passing prompts or recent frames between shots does not provide a shared spatial frame for revisiting a scene or a time-indexed record for replaying an earlier event. We present Any Time, Any Space (ATAS), which compiles a screenplay into shot requests with explicit location, camera, and event-time references. Before generation, it retrieves relevant evidence from a scene memory of registered static geometry and an event memory of dynamic observations indexed by scene, event, and time. After generation, ATAS reconstructs and registers the clip. It updates memory only when review and state checks accept the observation, while film delivery remains independent of memory admission. Across four video generators, ATAS improves all reported cross-shot consistency measures and achieves the highest aggregate benchmark score for each backbone. It also outperforms the compared agents and 3D-guided methods on target-image spatial revisit, leads three event-recapture criteria, and receives the highest human ratings on all six criteria. The video results and code are available on our project page: https://lhmd.top/atas/.

## 2. [Beyond Domain-Routed Supervision: Cross-Teacher Guarding for Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13521)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.13521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13521)

**Organization**: jingdong

**Authors**: Shi-Yu Tian, Yong Liu, Ximan Liu, Tianrui Zhu, Sule Bai, Xinwei Yang, Ruiqi Liu, Kun-Yang Yu, Ming Yang, Zhi Zhou, Yu-Feng Li, Yipeng Sun, Gu Simiu

**Published**: 2026-10-09 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Shi-Yu Tian

**Upvotes**: 138

### Abstract

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) extends on-policy distillation (OPD) by routing domain-specific data to specialized teachers, integrating their capabilities through dense token-level supervision on student-generated trajectories. However, domain routing only distinguishes the supervision sources for different domains; updates from all domains still act on shared parameters. When teachers differ substantially in response style and domain expertise, supervision that is effective in isolation may still lead to persistent gradient conflicts and capability degradation during joint optimization. To address this challenge, we propose Cross-Teacher Guarding for MOPD (Road MOPD), which introduces non-routed teachers as auxiliary cross-domain critics on the same student trajectories and coordinates multi-teacher supervision through token-level credit assignment and gradient updates. Specifically, cross-teacher-guided credit assignment uses teacher agreement to strengthen token-level corrections jointly supported by the teachers and attenuate conflicting signals; preventive multi-teacher gradient projection uses auxiliary gradients from the same trajectories to constrain potentially conflicting updates and adaptively controls projection strength to retain target-domain learning signals. In experiments, Road MOPD mitigates degradation in the disadvantaged domain, gains over 5 percentage points under high conflict, and improves overall performance in low-conflict and three-domain settings.

## 3. [Player-1: Learning to Play from Synthetic Experience](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20584)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.20584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20584)

**Organization**: ARC Lab, Tencent

**Authors**: Mingyu Ouyang, Yiran Wang, Siyuan Hu, Xingyilang Yin, Huiqiang Sun, Guangzhi Wang, Junfu Pu, Cheng Cheng, Honghao Chen, Ying Shan, Mike Zheng Shou, Hwee Tou Ng

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by yyyang404

**Upvotes**: 126

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/TencentARC/Player-1) | [Project Page](https://player-1-project.github.io/)

### Abstract

Computer-use agents have demonstrated strong capabilities in interpreting screens and executing keyboard and mouse actions. However, games, with their visually rich worlds and diverse mechanics, remain challenging and offer a demanding next step toward general digital control. Current game-agent learning pipelines often rely on large-scale demonstrations with limited coverage of dynamic game states, while scalable environments for interactive training remain difficult to obtain. We introduce Player-1, a post-training framework for learning game control beyond demonstrations through synthetic experience. We extend the computer-use action space with relative pointer motion, sustained inputs, and composite actions. For supervised initialization, we fine-tune computer-use models on 6,044 curated gameplay trajectories that pair recorded actions with task instructions and visual reasoning. We construct Player-1-Gym, sixteen synthetic 2D and 3D environments designed around control demands shared across games. Programmatically verifiable rewards and randomized observations and control dynamics support multi-environment reinforcement learning with PPO. The resulting Player-1-9B and Player-1-35B-A3B improve MineDojo-300 success over their SFT policies by 27.8 and 17.7 percentage points, respectively. Player-1-9B reaches 38.33% success compared with 12.63% for the strongest evaluated baseline, with further gains over SFT on ViZDoom-10 and GameWorld. Further analyses show that broader synthetic environment coverage improves transfer, while the use of composite actions that combine multiple keyboard and mouse inputs increases during RL. We demonstrate that learning from synthetic experience substantially improves game control beyond supervised initialization, with gains that generalize beyond the RL training suite.

## 4. [Doc2Harness: Benchmarking LLMs on Long-Horizon Harness Engineering of Coding Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20344)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.20344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20344)

**Organization**: Institute of Computing Technology

**Authors**: Yuyao Ge, Shenghua Liu, Peizhong Gao, Chendi Ge, Chenxu Liu, Yiwei Wang, Lingrui Mei, Baolong Bi, Jiayu Yao, Jiafeng Guo, Xueqi Cheng

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by YuyaoGe

**Upvotes**: 83

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/YuyaoGe/doc2harness) | [Project Page](https://geyuyao.com/doc2harness/)

### Abstract

Agents driven by large language models are increasingly deployed on long-horizon tasks, where they use tools and modify real workspaces. How well they perform depends as much on the harness around the model as on the model itself. Existing benchmarks measure downstream task success under a fixed harness, leaving harness engineering itself unexplored. We propose Doc2Harness, a two-stage benchmark that evaluates long-horizon harness engineering directly. In Stage 1, a build model operating inside a fixed builder harness receives an interface-only contract skeleton and a development document that consolidates behavior across twelve frontier harnesses, and then constructs a complete candidate harness in an isolated container. In Stage 2, the frozen candidate harness runs in containers separate from the construction environment, one per test case, and is driven end to end by a scripted mock model. We score each test case using four observable channels, namely the requests sent to the model, the files on disk, the process exit code, and the command output; positive controls rule out vacuous passes. Across nineteen frontier models, each evaluated with three independent builds on 328 test cases, GPT-6 Astra achieves the highest pass rate at 53.05%, while the weakest model reaches 27.44%, a gap of roughly twenty-six percentage points. Even the strongest model fails nearly half of the test cases, and failures cluster around how cross-cutting functionality is split across modules. To our knowledge, Doc2Harness provides the first deterministic benchmark for measuring long-horizon harness engineering.

## 5. [Recovering Reliable Supervision via Critique-Guided On-Policy Distillation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20572)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.20572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20572)

**Authors**: Kaixiang Zhao, Tianrun Yu, Shangzhe Li, Yuxiao Yang, Amanda Hughes, Weitong Zhang, Taylor W. Killian

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Kaixiang Zhao

**Upvotes**: 61

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/kzhao5/CriticOPD) | [Project Page](https://kzhao5.github.io/CriticOPD-website/)

### Abstract

On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student model on its own rollouts with token-level supervision from a teacher, assuming that the teacher can reach a correct answer from any prefix the student writes. We call this property the recoverability of the teacher and show it fails on incorrect rollouts: the teacher follows student errors, although it recognizes them. We propose CriticOPD, using error recognition to restore recoverability. The teacher critiques an incorrect rollout, allowing the student to rewrite its solution following the error. The resulting output, including the critique as privileged information, is scored by the teacher while the student uses the revision without the critique for training. On two Qwen3 students and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, CriticOPD improves OPD, with the largest gains on competition problems.

## 6. [LatentLoop: Scaling Looped MoE Models with Asymmetric Compute Allocation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19975)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.19975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19975)

**Authors**: Zefeng He, Yunkai Chai, Xiaoyang Chen, Tong Zhu, Zongliang Wu, Yun Luo, Yafu Li, Yu Cheng

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Zefeng He

**Upvotes**: 56

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/lcqysl/LatentLoop) | [Project Page](https://latentloop-project.github.io/)

### Abstract

Looped Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models combine parameter reuse with sparse computation to improve performance under matched budgets. Prior work demonstrates that different loop visits can play different roles even when they share parameters. To probe the contributions of different loop visits, we learn separate residual coefficients in a model that applies a shared block twice, observing a smaller scale in the first visit. Fixed coefficient experiments show that reducing the first visit's scale preserves or improves performance, whereas the same reductions to the second hurt it. This asymmetry motivates us to explore asymmetric compute allocation. We propose LatentLoop, which compresses the first visit's sequence, retains full resolution in the second, and reallocates saved computation to increase the hidden size. We further learn how strongly the first visit updates the full sequence, and share MoE weights across visits while using separate attention parameters to accommodate their different sequence resolutions. Across 15 configurations spanning 1.3B--7B parameters, with the largest budget exceeding 10^{21} FLOPs, LatentLoop consistently lowers validation NLL. At a fitted NLL of 1.988, predicted compute savings are 19.0\% and 7.9\% relative to vanilla MoE and the looped baseline. It also improves average scores on nine benchmarks at all sizes in 0-shot and 5-shot settings. These results suggest asymmetric compute allocation is a promising direction for looped MoE models.

## 7. [Cortex: Consolidating Context into Parametric Memory](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20399)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.20399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20399)

**Organization**: ByteDance Seed

**Authors**: Jintian Zhang, Feng He, Xinnian Liang, Ziwen Xu, Haoming Xu, Haojing Huang, Shuangzhi Wu, Ningyu Zhang

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Ningyu Zhang

**Upvotes**: 53

### Abstract

Context can improve large language model performance, but these gains may be lost after context removal. Inspired by biological systems consolidation, we formulate Memory Consolidation (MC) as internalizing these gains while preserving non-target capabilities, connecting context-specified editing, skill learning, and behavioral steering under a common objective. We introduce a five-task-family testbed measuring consolidation and retention under single, batch, and sequential updates. We propose Cortex, which generates prospective queries to anticipate uses of context and distills context-conditioned outputs into mergeable low-rank or structural feed-forward network updates. Across 4B--120B dense and mixture-of-experts backbones, including both text-only and multimodal models, Cortex lies on the empirical consolidation--retention Pareto frontier in most evaluated settings. After 50 sequential factual edits on a 14B model, Cortex-Structural recovers 88.9% of the context-induced gain while retaining 99.8% of original non-target performance.

## 8. [ActionOPD: Distilling Decisions, Not Tokens](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19820)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.19820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19820)

**Organization**: Tsinghua-NICS-EFC

**Authors**: Huining Yuan, Xiangmin Yi, Zelai Xu, Zixu Wang, Xinlei Chen, Xiao-Ping Zhang, Yu Wang, Chao Yu

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Huining Yuan

**Upvotes**: 46

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/thu-nics/ActionOPD) | [Project Page](https://thu-nics.github.io/ActionOPD/)

### Abstract

Distilling capable language-model agents into smaller models requires aligning the student's behavior with the teacher's. This raises a fundamental question: at which states and at what granularity should this alignment be performed? Offline imitation provides dense but token-specific supervision and can suffer from state-distribution mismatch. Token-level on-policy distillation (OPD) addresses this mismatch but typically requires teacher token probabilities and compatible tokenization. We argue that semantic actions provide a natural unit of supervision for agent distillation. We introduce ActionOPD, an action-level on-policy distillation method motivated by maximizing expected teacher-action likelihood at student-encountered states. At each such state, ActionOPD samples actions from both the student and a black-box teacher, rewarding student actions according to their semantic agreement with the sampled teacher actions. Through policy optimization, ActionOPD aligns the student's action-level behavior with the teacher's decisions without requiring reproduction of teacher-written reasoning or access to teacher token probabilities. Training Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B with two teachers on diverse, curated tool-use tasks yields average gains of 3.3-6.9 percentage points across four out-of-distribution benchmarks. ActionOPD scales favorably with student size and teacher capability, without requiring the teacher and student to share a model family or tokenizer. On τ^2-bench, ActionOPD achieves competitive performance against offline distillation and token-level OPD. We further explore Self-ActionOPD with a same-model teacher and privileged context. These results establish ActionOPD as a promising approach to agent distillation.

## 9. [More Methods, Better Agents? A Reality Check on Self-Distillation in Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19789)

### Abstract

Self-distillation in agentic reinforcement learning has produced a growing number of methods. However, existing evaluations often reuse published baseline scores under differing experimental conditions, provide few direct comparisons among contemporaneous methods, and give limited attention to training stability and computational cost. These limitations obscure the source of reported gains and the practical strengths and weaknesses of competing methods. Costly agent training further motivates a common basis for comparison. To address these gaps, we compare ten representative methods against GRPO on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, Search-QA, and WebShop across three model sizes. Our study comprises 132 training runs totaling 12,124.4 recorded H800 GPU-hours, with repeated evaluations over eight rollout seeds for the pretrained backbones and all eleven trained methods. Beyond final task performance, we examine training progress, stability, and measured computational cost. Our evaluation yields three key findings. First, gains over GRPO remain limited in scope, with no method consistently outperforming it across settings. Second, strong final performance can mask unstable training. Third, additional training cost does not consistently translate into better performance. We establish this benchmark to support fair, comprehensive comparison and guide future research toward reliable and cost-effective improvements in agent training. ## 16. [Super-Resolution in The Right Latent Space: A Frozen Vision-Foundation Substrate](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04781)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.04781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04781)

**Organization**: RTP-LLM

**Authors**: Wanzhou Lei, Cuifeng Shen, Yanjin He, Maohua Li, Hua Yuan, Per-Olof Persson, Tao Lan, Kan Liu, Hanlin Tang

**Published**: 2026-10-08 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Maohua Li

**Upvotes**: 13

### Abstract

In an image latent space, the embeddings of high-resolution, natural, and sharp images form a manifold. Degradation of high-resolution images pushes their embeddings off this manifold. Real-world super-resolution (SR) then becomes the task of mapping the degraded embedding back onto this manifold --- not anywhere on the manifold, but to the point that preserves what the input still carries, both its semantics and pixel details. Every published method implements this mapping in a reconstruction-oriented latent space or pixel space. We claim these spaces are the wrong substrates for SR. Low-resolution and degraded images are embedded far from the manifold, making the mapping difficult and expensive. The lack of semantic information in these substrates also makes it difficult to navigate to the faithful point on the manifold, causing severe hallucination when degradation is heavy. ### Abstract

Multimodal LLMs are typically trained by first aligning a vision encoder with a language model via projector training on large image-caption datasets, followed by joint training of the full multimodal model. Despite its widespread use, the role of the projector training stage remains poorly understood. We find that (i) using only 10 to 20% of the data for training the projector already recovers most downstream performance gains, and (ii) projectors trained on different datasets can be linearly interpolated while largely preserving performance. These results suggest that projector training mainly performs a coarse alignment, which many different projectors achieve equally well. Motivated by these findings, we propose PORTAL, a training-free projector initialization that requires neither paired image-caption data nor gradient-based optimization, but only per-modality summary statistics. PORTAL computes an optimal-transport-based map between Gaussian approximations of the vision and language feature distributions on a shared principal subspace, so that the projector training stage can be skipped entirely. Without any paired data, PORTAL matches the standard pipeline trained on 558k image-caption pairs across six LLM backbones, two vision encoders, and 16 benchmarks. It outperforms that pipeline in 3 of 7 (vision encoder, LLM) settings and reaches an average 98% of its score across the other four.

## 50. [Rethinking LLM-Guided Visual Question Answering: Does Deeper Knowledge Integration Really Help?](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17079)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.17079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17079)

**Authors**: Aneerban Saha, Vijay Kumar, Sameer Madhav D., Priyam Srivastava, Dhruv Bansal

**Published**: 2026-10-12 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-15 by Aneerban Saha

**Upvotes**: 1

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/rishii100/Rethinking-LLM-Guided-Visual-Question-Answering-Does-Deeper-Knowledge-Integration-Really-Help) | [Project Page](https://huggingface.co/rishii100/knowledge-guided-vqa-models)

### Abstract

Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering (VQA) requires reasoning that extends beyond visible image content, demanding external commonsense and factual knowledge. While recent work leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) as knowledge sources, the question of how deeply such guidance should be integrated into a vision language architecture remains underexplored. In this work, we generate structured reasoning guidance, visual focus concepts, distractor elements and external knowledge using Llama~3.3~70B and systematically compare two integration strategies within a unified frozen ViT + FLAN-T5 framework on the OK-VQA benchmark: decision level late fusion and attention-matrix injection. Contrary to the prevailing assumption that deeper integration is superior, we find that shallow late fusion achieves the strongest results (39.4% exact match, +10.0 over the no guidance baseline), outperforming deep attention modification (31.8%) under data scarce fine-tuning. We further contribute an LLM based augmentation pipeline that expands training data more than tenfold via question paraphrasing. Our findings indicate that integration depth must be matched to the data regime.
