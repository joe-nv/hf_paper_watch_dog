## 1. [Scaling Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Brain Encoding](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26802)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.26802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26802)

**Organization**: Mind Lab

**Authors**: Zhuofan Josh Ying, Guanglong Sun, Nolan Ho, Kieran Liu, Rui Yang, Andrew Chen, Nikolaus Kriegeskorte, Pony Ma, Hossein Adeli

**Published**: 2026-10-19 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Xiaoteng Ma

**Upvotes**: 107

### Abstract

Brain encoding models aim to predict neural responses to stimuli. The most widely used approach fits readouts from frozen pretrained backbones. Using neural data to train large models from scratch or fully fine-tune them after task-pretraining has proven difficult because neural data is costly and limited. We show that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) enables us to effectively adapt very large pretrained backbones for brain encoding with a small number of trainable parameters. To make it computationally practical, we exploit the backbone's causal structure to predict multiple timepoints in a single pass instead of using sliding windows, significantly reducing feature-extraction cost. This enables efficient fine-tuning of multimodal backbones with up to 104B parameters, the largest model adapted for brain encoding to our knowledge. Trained with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) and a lightweight readout, our approach consistently improves brain encoding performance across nine diverse multimodal backbones. On the same Qwen3-Omni backbone as the prior state-of-the-art (SoTA), our method BrainLoRA achieves the best published performance on the Algonauts 2025 Challenge. We also show the adapted multimodal backbones remain interpretable: modality attribution recovers the canonical sensory topography that prior work often obtains by construction from separate unimodal encoders, and neural adaptation increases the contribution of audio inputs and cross-modal mixing within the backbone. To scale out the number of adapted models, we develop a multi-LoRA kernel that accelerates joint training of multiple LoRAs on a shared backbone. This enables an ensemble of 200+ adapted models that ranks first on the public Algonauts post-challenge leaderboard at the time of writing. Together, our results highlight PEFT as an efficient and powerful paradigm for brain encoding.

## 2. [Learning from Fleet Experience: A Collective Learning Paradigm for Robots](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25736)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.25736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25736)

**Authors**: Chao Yu, Shu&#39;ang Yu, Hao Lin, Hongzhi Zang, Yuxiang Yan, Chenxi Jiang, Zhen Guo, Chuankang Li, Yihao Chen, Xin Xu, Xu Fu, Jiakai Zhou, Liangzhi Shi, Hongyi Ren, Yiwei Liu, Jing Wu, Yuanqing Wang, Chen Wu, Bo Dai, Chunyu Yang, Hongliang Huang, Shuoyu Yue, Weiqi Yin, Yinuo Chen, Ruihua Chen, Chunyang Zhu, Han Xiao, Yanghao Li, Ruipu Wu, Zhikai Cai, Xiu Chen, Xu Tan, Yuchen Yan, Jialin Zhang, Wen Zeng, Yiming Zhou, Zixiao Huang, Guanghui Ren, Quanlu Zhang, Boxun Li, Maoqing Yao, Yu Wang

**Published**: 2026-10-19 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Shu'ang Yu

**Upvotes**: 99

**Links**: [Project Page](https://cel-robot-learning.github.io)

### Abstract

Experience generated during deployment offers an opportunity for robots to improve policy reliability through learning from their own executions. However, learning through human corrections requires teleoperators to monitor execution and intervene when policies fail, a demand that is difficult to meet as robot fleets grow. We introduce collective experience learning (CEL), a learning paradigm in which deployed robots share their execution experience to improve a common policy. We realize CEL through failure-guided experience retrieval fine-tuning (FERT), which uses failure contexts from robot executions to retrieve relevant trajectory segments from the fleet's shared successful experience and uses their actions as supervision for policy updates, without collecting additional expert demonstrations or corrective actions. A fault-tolerant asynchronous training system (FATS) coordinates experience collection, policy optimization and policy distribution despite local interruptions within the robot fleet. Across five real-world manipulation tasks, the resulting policies achieve success rates of 93.3-99.2%, evaluated over 120 trials per task on robots excluded from training data collection. With collection time per robot held fixed, larger fleets provide more experience and FERT generally achieves higher success rates across the evaluated sizes. On multi-stage phone packaging, learning with 96 robots increases success from 79.2% to 98.3%. These results support collective experience as a practical basis for scalable robot learning and a path towards improving policy reliability during deployment without collecting additional expert corrective actions.

### Abstract

Memory-native evolution requires making memory an intrinsic capability of foundation models, enabling them to continuously accumulate and utilize information. To this end, we introduce MemT-1.0, a new architecture of memory foundation models that internalize memory as part of model computation. Building on Metis, MemT-1.0 employs Gated DeltaNet-2 with a stable token-level memory update mechanism within each turn. We further introduce Mixture-of-Storage to scale native memory capacity. Following a deliberate backbone preparation, we develop a two-stage training pipeline consisting of continued pre-training and post-training, supported by 34B tokens of training data and efficient training and inference infrastructure. The results show that they outperform the parametric memory baselines on average and remain competitive with retrieval-augmented methods, while largely preserving general capabilities. At a history length of 128K tokens, MemT-1.0-Nano achieves approximately 1.86times end-to-end inference speedup and a 66times reduction in persistent storage relative to full context KV cache. By internalizing retrieved procedural experience, MemT-1.0 improves task success on agentic tasks, providing a practical step toward memory-native evolution.

## 5. [EgoXperience: Learning World-Grounded Egocentric Representations from Human Experience](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26625)

### Abstract

Reconstructing human activity from egocentric video requires more than recovering individual 3D components: the observer's motion, surrounding geometry, and hand-object interactions must fit together in a consistent world. Achieving this coherence requires both joint reconstruction and aligned supervision, yet existing methods and datasets offer limited support for either. We present EgoXperience, a full-stack paradigm that addresses these needs through data collection, automatic annotation, unified modeling, and benchmarking. Central to EgoXperience is an automatic annotation engine, EgoX-Engine, that couples geometrically verified object tracking with SLAM-based world alignment, yielding consistent supervision for the camera, scene, hands, and objects. This yields EgoX-Data-6M, a 50-hour dataset of task-driven manipulation and locomotion within and across indoor and outdoor environments, with second-level task captions. To translate this supervision into reconstruction, we introduce EgoX, trained with EgoX-Data-6M and complementary annotations from additional public datasets. Given monocular egocentric video and first-frame object masks, our model achieves joint metric reconstruction of the camera, scene, hands, and objects in a shared world coordinate frame with a single forward pass. Extensive experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across scene estimation and world-space hand-object pose recovery. Against the strongest evaluated baselines, EgoX reduces camera, hand, and depth errors by 68.9%, 65.6%, and 42.9%, and improves object pose scores by 28.0%.

### Abstract

We study language-conditioned local navigation, where a robot must navigate to a text-specified target visible in its initial observation and stop at the described location. End-to-end navigation policies require diverse training data to generalize across scenes, yet real-robot data is costly to collect and simulated data leaves a sim-to-real gap. Internet egocentric videos offer a scalable source of real-world navigation experience. ## 7. [OpenVLHarness: Real World Visual Agentic Tool-Use with Multimodal Memory](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26860)

### Abstract

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made rapid progress on visual reasoning, yet many tasks require evidence that is difficult to obtain through internal reasoning alone: our controlled study on 22 benchmarks shows that simply increasing the inference budget of the base model yields only modest gains. Such tasks demand grounded perception, external knowledge, and numerical measurements obtained through heterogeneous tools, whose outputs must be made interpretable to the model and retained across reasoning steps. We introduce OpenVLHarness, a multimodal reasoning harness organized around a capability layer and persistent multimodal memory. The capability layer combines task-level tool interfaces with evidence rendering, providing consistent access to heterogeneous backends and converting their outputs into model-readable observations. ## 8. [SpecMRL: Redesign Matryoshka Representation Learning within Parametric Spectrum](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26824)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.26824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26824)

**Authors**: Zhongxin Chen, Songhao Wu, Heng Cui, Cong Li, Xiao Zhou, Rui Yan

**Published**: 2026-10-19 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Songhao Wu

**Upvotes**: 60

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/CentreChen/Spec-MRL)

### Abstract

Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) is designed to support flexible representation sizes by jointly optimizing nested prefixes. While effective, this design lacks an explicit constraint to ensure that the leading portion provides a suitable compact encoding. To address this, we propose SpecMRL, which reorganizes the feature space to enable more effective prefix readout. Specifically, we extend MRL with a parameter-derived spectral basis organized with feature importance. SpecMRL otherwise follows standard MRL, with prefix readout performed in the learned spectral space. Experiments on MTEB show that SpecMRL outperforms MRL at all evaluated reduced dimensions, with larger gains under more aggressive compression. These results suggest that the learned spectral basis better prioritizes informative directions, enabling more effective representation compression. Notably, SpecMRL extends the performance frontier of MRL, surpassing full-dimensional MRL with only half the embedding dimensions.

## 10. [Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22791)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.22791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791)

**Organization**: Microsoft Research

**Authors**: Zhepei Wei, Wenlin Yao, Baolin Peng, Danqing Wang, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng, Yu Meng, Jianfeng Gao

**Published**: 2026-10-16 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Zhepei Wei

**Upvotes**: 36

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/MSR-Orchard/Self-HPO) | [Project Page](https://weizhepei.com/blog/self-hpo/)

### Abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents have shown promising potential in coding tasks, and their performance is jointly determined by the policy model and the harness that mediates their interaction with the environment. Indeed, harness and policy are mutually reinforcing and evolving only one component is suboptimal---a harness tuned for one checkpoint may not target the failure modes of an updated policy, while policy learning remains limited to the trajectories elicited by a fixed harness. In light of this, we propose Self-HPO, a recursive self-improving framework for coding agents that alternates harness and policy optimization, using the evolving policy as its own harness proposer. Specifically, in each round, the current policy first analyzes its own failures and proposes new harness candidates (H-step). The policy is then updated through reinforcement learning (RL) under the selected harness (P-step), and hard tasks identified during training form the next harness search set, keeping harness optimization targeted to the policy's evolving failure modes. Across three coding benchmarks, Self-HPO consistently outperforms harness-only and policy-only baselines, improving Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate from 57.8% to 64.8%, with gains generalizing to out-of-distribution settings. Further analysis shows that both optimized components retain gains even when paired with their base counterparts, while their joint gain exceeds the sum of their individual gains. Self-HPO also improves reliability (Pass@1) and expands the capability frontier (Pass@k) over the base agent. Additionally, we show that with a stronger external model (e.g., Opus 4.8) as the harness proposer, the same framework yields a further performance boost.

## 14. [RoAR-VAE: Improving Latent Diffusability through Robust Prediction](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26216)

### Abstract

An effective visual tokenizer must preserve image fidelity while producing latents that downstream diffusion models can learn efficiently. Prior work improves latent diffusability through semantic alignment or joint tokenizer--diffusion training. Revisiting REPA-E, a representative approach, we find that removing its diffusion objective and dedicated generative blocks while retaining semantic prediction from corrupted latents yields comparable downstream generation performance under a matched setting. ## 15. [Interlock: From Local Correctness to Task Completion in Long-Horizon Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19686)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.19686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19686)

**Organization**: LongCat

**Authors**: Linji Hao, Yanji He, Zihan Wang, Hansi Yang, Jiayuan Zhang, Hongyan Hao, Qi Gu, Yan Feng, Han Wu

**Published**: 2026-10-14 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Linji Hao

**Upvotes**: 23

### Abstract

Large language model agents increasingly undertake long-horizon tasks in complex environments characterized by fragmented evidence, partially completed states, and dependencies across resources and artifacts. Existing agents may satisfy individual requirements while failing to maintain these relationships, producing locally correct but incomplete outcomes. These failures arise because task completion depends on relationships among environmental evidence, consequential decisions, and required effects. To address this completion gap, we propose Interlock, a framework for task synthesis that represents each task as a typed completion graph. Grounding edges connect public evidence to decisions, coupling hyperedges impose joint constraints across components, and realization edges connect decisions to all required downstream effects. Interlock compiles this graph into a resettable workspace, a public request, and an evaluation rubric. **ArXiv ID**: [2610.21810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21810)

**Organization**: Dnotitia Inc.

**Authors**: Donghoon Han, Minseop Jung, Dong Woo Kwak, SeungJae Lee, Junhoo Lee

**Published**: 2026-10-16 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Junhoo

**Upvotes**: 2

### Abstract

Conditional memory expands language-model capacity through selective lookup, but the full embedding table must remain available for every possible input. We introduce Conditional Program (CP), a compressed representation that reconstructs only the rows an input requests. CP represents each row by a short code and a local scale, with a reconstruction program shared across the table. We instantiate this representation with a Gaussian trellis, norm restoration and independently decodable row records. CP's two-bit code lowers output KL by 41--63\% relative to 2-bit round-to-nearest. Analysis across three trained n-gram memory families shows that Gaussian-like coordinate statistics coexist with learned relationships among rows; at matched storage budgets, the shared trellis outperforms the fitted codebooks and low-rank residual variants we test. For DeepSeek, it reduces table storage from 203GB to 51GB, leaving room for larger workloads when the table is kept on GPU.

## 49. [ECHO: Efficient Causal Self-Recursion Beyond Auxiliary Multi-Token Prediction](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24766)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.24766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24766)

**Organization**: ernie-research

**Authors**: Yuxuan Zhou, Junyuan Shang, Tianyu Wang, Chunrui Zheng, Zichang Liu, Wenbin Chang, Shuohuan Wang, Dianhai Yu

**Published**: 2026-10-17 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Tianyu Wang

**Upvotes**: 2

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/ernie-research/ECHO)

### Abstract

Existing Multi-token prediction (MTP) methods are auxiliary by design: the multi-horizon loss mostly trains modules attached to the backbone, and each added horizon costs another module and another pass. We propose Efficient Causal Self-Recursion (ECHO), which removes both costs. The backbone's own final layer predicts every horizon, so the supervision lands on the parameters that generate the next token, and Dynamic Depth Sampling (DDS) keeps deeper horizons affordable by drawing the training depth with probabilities that decay along depth. Future prediction thus becomes part of the autoregressive objective rather than a task attached to it, and the gains follow on both sides of it. In pretraining, added depth converts into downstream accuracy where auxiliary modules saturate, concentrating on reasoning and code at half the MTP training overhead of training every depth. At inference, the pretrained model is its own drafter: it drafts beyond the depth it was trained for, and keeps its draft-target alignment on the open-ended, high-entropy generation where post-hoc drafters lose theirs. The advantage survives a doubled token budget, a repeated pretraining run, and supervised finetuning.

## 50. [Text-to-Image Generative Modeling Without Language Pretraining](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26863)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.26863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26863)

**Organization**: Stanford University

**Authors**: Keshigeyan Chandrasegaran, Yue Zhao, Armin W. Thomas, Ehsan Adeli, Jiajun Wu, Juan Carlos Niebles, Li Fei-Fei, Michael Poli

**Published**: 2026-10-19 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-20 by Keshigeyan Chandrasegaran

**Upvotes**: 2

**Links**: [Project Page](http://cs.stanford.edu/~keshik/halcyon)

### Abstract

Modern text-to-image (T2I) systems use internet-scale language pretraining for text-conditioned image generation. We ask whether language pretraining is necessary for strong T2I generation. We introduce Halcyon, a family of early-fusion multimodal autoregressive models pretrained entirely from scratch on GPIC, a dataset of 100M image-text pairs. Halcyon-0.52B outperforms LlamaGen-XL/8 (48.0 vs. 63.3 gFD) with 18.2times less pretraining compute. Further, Halcyon-1.6B outperforms JiT-T2I-XXL/8++ (35.3 vs. 41.0 gFD) with 275times less pretraining compute. Controlled experiments show that text-only pretraining does not improve T2I generation. Halcyon also learns image captioning by replacing caption dropout with I2T supervision, maintaining competitive T2I generation quality under the same pretraining budget. We show that strong T2I systems can be trained from scratch without internet-scale language pretraining. Code and models are available at https://cs.stanford.edu/~keshik/halcyon
