## 1. [DTCA: Denoising Token Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning in Diffusion Language Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35201)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.35201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35201)

**Organization**: ZJU-OmniAI

**Authors**: Hongze Chen, Miao Pan, Lizhou Liang, Qinfeng Li, Xinyin Ma, Yongliang Shen, Weishi Wang, Daniel Dahlmeier, Xuhong Zhang, Wenqi Zhang

**Published**: 2026-10-26 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Wenqi Zhang

**Upvotes**: 256

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/ZJU-OmniAI/DTCA) | [Project Page](https://zju-omniai.github.io/DTCA/)

### Abstract

Diffusion language models (DLMs) generate text through iterative denoising, enabling bidirectional context modeling and parallel token refinement. Recent studies have applied reinforcement learning (RL) to DLMs and improved their reasoning performance. However, the iterative denoising process makes credit assignment more challenging, as final outcome rewards provide limited information about how different tokens contribute to the final output. In this paper, we propose Denoising Token Credit Assignment (DTCA), a framework for token-level credit assignment in reinforcement learning for diffusion language models. DTCA estimates token contributions by measuring the improvement in prediction confidence of remaining masked positions after token commitment and groups these signals according to part-of-speech (POS) categories. These contribution weights are used to select informative denoising timesteps and assign token-level learning signals during RL training. DTCA further calibrates these learning signals according to token-level entropy changes induced by policy updates. Experiments on LLaDA-8B-Instruct show that DTCA consistently improves mathematical reasoning performance across different generation budgets, with gains of up to 9.0 points on MATH500 and 5.3 points on GSM8K over the base model.

## 2. [Efficient Streaming Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction for Real-World Acoustic Scenes](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32749)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.32749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32749)

**Organization**: Tsinghua Machine Learning Group

**Authors**: Wendi Sang, Kai Li, Yifan Li, Jianqiang Huang, Xiaolin Hu

**Published**: 2026-10-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Kai Li

**Upvotes**: 146

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/JusperLee/Falcon) | [Project Page](https://cslikai.cn/Falcon/)

### Abstract

Streaming audio-visual target speaker extraction (AVTSE) is essential for latency-sensitive applications. However, current causal systems face two coupled gaps. First, standard benchmarks fix the number of speakers at two and ignore the near-/far-field interference, reverberation, and sudden noise of real scenes. Second, high-performance causal architectures are too heavy for edge deployment. Lightweight alternatives try to reclaim capacity by repeatedly invoking a small separator, which offsets the savings. To address the first gap, we release RealSSA, a realistic AVTSE benchmark consisting of RealSSA-Sim and RealSSA-Real. RealSSA-Sim provides controllable simulated scenes with 1--5 dynamic near-field interfering speakers; scenes with far-field interference additionally include 10--15 far-field interfering speakers. RealSSA-Real provides a held-out real-recorded evaluation set. To address the second gap, we propose Falcon, a causal AVTSE method. Its Separator performs multi-scale time-frequency separation in a single encoder--decoder pass. We train Falcon with an encoder-space speaker contrastive loss. This loss suppresses near-field leakage at zero inference cost and transfers across backbones. Falcon achieves state-of-the-art extraction quality on RealSSA-Sim, LRS2-2Mix, LRS3-2Mix, and VoxCeleb2-2Mix. Compared with the causal AV-TFGridNet baseline, it cuts parameters by 91.9%, computation by 20.2 times, and GPU inference latency by 11.1 times. Code and demo are available at https://cslikai.cn/Falcon/.

## 3. [OmniShowcase: Omnidirectional Subject-Consistent Video Generation from Multi-View Images](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35684)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.35684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35684)

**Organization**: KAIST AI

**Authors**: Junyeong Ahn, Geonwoo Kim, Kinam Kim, Gihwi Kim, Hoonjin Jung, Hyojin Jang, Jaegul Choo

**Published**: 2026-10-26 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Junyeong Ahn

**Upvotes**: 39

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/justin4ai/OmniShowcase) | [Project Page](https://justin4ai.github.io/OmniShowcase/)

### Abstract

Generating videos of a specific subject from a few reference images remains challenging when the camera reveals surfaces that are not visible in the input. Early subject-to-video (S2V) methods condition on a single reference image and must therefore infer the subject's unseen appearance and geometry as the viewpoint changes. To address this limitation, recent methods extend S2V to multiple reference views, providing more information about the subject from different viewpoints. However, they do not explicitly specify the 3D structure that connects these observations. In addition, existing training data do not guarantee that viewpoint changes preserve the same underlying 3D subject. We present OmniShowcase, built on two components: (i) a fully automated data synthesis pipeline that produces training videos along diverse camera trajectories, with viewpoint variation driven by underlying subject geometry, even spanning full 360-degree orbits; and (ii) a geometry-conditioning method that provides the video model with a turntable of surface-normal maps reconstructed from the reference views without requiring temporal alignment with the generated frames. We construct Showcase-4K, a corpus of 3D-asset-based videos covering twelve camera motions across 88 trajectory configurations with reconstruction-derived surface-normal conditioning, and ShowcaseBench, a held-out evaluation set with the source meshes and camera parameters used to render each video. Experiments show state-of-the-art subject identity and 3D consistency while maintaining competitive video quality and motion. Project page: justin4ai.github.io/OmniShowcase

## 4. [Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31670)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.31670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31670)

**Organization**: Rice University

**Authors**: Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei

**Published**: 2026-10-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Yunfei Xie

**Upvotes**: 33

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/yunfeixie233/sensorimotor-alignment) | [Project Page](https://yunfeixie233.github.io/s2/)

### Abstract

Robot foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, motivating low-cost offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To test this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment (S^2), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that S^2 correlates positively with downstream robot task success across model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. S^2 can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, S^2 offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With S^2, we extend PRH to robotics and make sensorimotor alignment measurable, offering a low-cost, representation-level view of robot foundation models that closed-loop success rates alone cannot reveal.

## 5. [RealCLI: Toward a Unified Evaluation of Human-Agent Collaboration for CLI Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22507)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.22507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22507)

**Organization**: IQuest

**Authors**: Yunqi Liu, Peiyu Wang, Bingli Wang, Leo Luo, Xuanwu Song, Xudong Ma, Hongxin Li, Yaxin Du, Weicheng Gu, Fanglin Xu, Yi Xing, XiangLong Liu, Jujie He, Zhange Zhang, Huatong Song, Zhaoyi Li, Deyang Kong, Chuan Hao, Peihao Wu, Mingjie Tang, Ran Tao, Jian Yang, Wayne Xin Zhao, Bryan Dai

**Published**: 2026-10-15 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Jian Yang

**Upvotes**: 14

### Abstract

Command-line interface (CLI) agents increasingly execute longer-horizon tasks, yet existing benchmarks either assume that user instructions remain fixed throughout execution or evaluate task outcomes without capturing runtime collaboration quality. In this paper, we present REALCLI, a paired benchmark that unifies the evaluation of task execution and human-agent collaboration quality on realistic CLI tasks. To comprehensively measure collaboration, REALCLI builds a five-dimensional framework, operationalized into 22 sub-dimensions and 51 interaction scenario templates. REALCLI provides two modes for paired comparison: REALCLI-EXEC supplies complete specifications upfront, whereas REALCLI-COLLAB introduces a scripted user that delivers progressive information, corrections, constraint revisions, and authorization decisions. Evaluations reveal profound capability gaps in frontier agents: the best configuration attains an average score of only 49.2%, and when evaluated by the joint collaboration score (J_{collab}), the peak performance reaches merely 37.6%. These results show that task effectiveness diverges sharply from collaboration quality, with models consistently struggling in steerability, trust calibration, and safety boundary management. REALCLI provides a rigorous, reproducible foundation for measuring and advancing collaborative CLI agents.

## 6. [TAO: Tool-Aware Joint Scheduling for LLM-Based Agentic Serving](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37230)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.37230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37230)

**Organization**: AIOR

**Authors**: Shuhuai Li, Shengdong Ye, Dongdong Ge, Yinyu Ye, Jianghao Lin

**Published**: 2026-10-27 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Li shuhuai

**Upvotes**: 14

### Abstract

Agentic services alternate between model inference on clustered GPUs and external tools on distributed CPUs. In this scenario, existing systems (1) overlook tool-return readiness in service evaluation, wasting limited resources, and (2) use heuristic strategies to decide admission and placement on GPUs, resulting in suboptimal scheduling decisions. To bridge this gap, we present TAO, a tool-aware joint scheduling framework for LLM-based agentic serving. Its value interface combines near-term tool readiness, node-specific cache credit with time-aware decay, and token footprint; a dynamic program jointly selects and places waiting sessions under capacity constraints. Across the 12 reported model-workload-concurrency settings, TAO reduces latency by up to 26.8%, reduces mean time to first token (TTFT) by up to 36.3%, and increases decode throughput by up to 29.5% relative to the best evaluated state-of-the-art baseline for each metric.

## 7. [TauIndianBankBench: Procedure-Aware, Action-Oriented Evaluation of Tool-Using Conversational Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30161)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.30161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30161)

**Organization**: National Payments Corporation of India

**Authors**: Krishanu Adhikary, Prashant Devadiga

**Published**: 2026-09-30 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Krishanu Adhikary

**Upvotes**: 7

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/npci/TauIndianBankBench)

### Abstract

Many Indian banks serve customers through FAQ pages and scripted chatbots. Assistants based on language models could respond faster and carry out requests such as blocking a card, but only if they give correct information and follow the bank's rules. These rules prescribe a procedure, a set of required steps, such as obtaining a closure quote before closing a deposit. Adapting models to this domain requires measuring how well they follow the procedure, before and after training. Benchmarks usually judge only the records left at the end of a conversation. Many required steps, however, leave the records unchanged, so an assistant that skips them can still be judged successful. We present TauIndianBankBench, an action-oriented benchmark of 1,000 Indian retail-banking tasks where an assistant helps a simulated customer. Besides the final records, it checks that the assistant took every required step and made no unrequested change. For every model, 6.5-12.0% of sessions are judged successful when only the final records are checked, although the assistant skipped a required step or made an unrequested change. Scoring both procedure and outcome shows more accurately how well language models perform in Indian retail banking.

## 8. [LongZero: Enhancing Long-Context Reasoning in LLMs from Zero Data](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.30132)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.30132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30132)

**Organization**: Tsinghua University

**Authors**: Zhennan Wan, Jianfei Chen

**Published**: 2026-10-21 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Zhennan Wan

**Upvotes**: 2

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/zhennan1/LongZero)

### Abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) is an effective way to strengthen the long-context reasoning of large language models (LLMs), but it needs training data that are long, diverse, challenging, and verifiable. Human annotation of such data is expensive, naturally occurring data are too scarce to sustain RL, and existing synthesis methods depend on external corpora or stronger teacher models, which become harder to obtain as the trained models get stronger. Recently, RL from zero data, in which a model learns from tasks it proposes itself, has achieved excellent performance, but existing methods target short-context tasks. Therefore, we propose LongZero, a method that lets an LLM improve its own long-context reasoning without any external data. Instead of writing questions and answers over existing documents, LongZero first has the model synthesize the question and answer with its supporting evidence in a compact structured form, and only then separately expands the evidence into a long multi-document context. LongZero combines these self-written multi-hop reasoning tasks with self-written multi-round retrieval tasks and trains the model with GRPO. We evaluate LongZero on two LLMs across five long-context reasoning benchmarks and compare it with RL on data synthesized from external corpora. Experimental results show that LongZero can generalize to real tasks, and outperforms both baselines, suggesting a viable and promising path to improving long-context reasoning of LLMs.

## 9. [CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29702)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.29702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29702)

**Authors**: Yijun Shen, Diqi He, Jinbang Guo, Wanyue Zhang, Jiaming Mi, Huan Yang, Zhongyuan Wang, Zhiwei Yu, Kai Zhang

**Published**: 2026-10-21 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Yijun Shen

**Upvotes**: 2

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/Intelligent-Interaction/CausGround) | [Project Page](https://causground.github.io/)

### Abstract

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) show promising reasoning capabilities, but reliable physical interaction requires planning that accounts for when actions are executable and how they change the world. These preconditions and effects are environment-dependent and often not captured by MLLMs, making adaptation challenging. Yet existing approaches either require costly environment-specific training or rely on unstructured interaction experience that is difficult to reuse. Therefore, we introduce CausGround, a non-parametric causal interaction grounding framework that distills interaction experience into reusable action-centered causal knowledge. CausGround identifies action preconditions and effects and organizes them in a factorized world-state representation capturing object attributes and inter-object relations. At planning time, task-relevant causal dependencies are grounded in current observations to support backward reasoning over preconditions and forward reasoning over effects for action selection and multi-step planning. We evaluate CausGround on simulated high-level planning, interaction reasoning, and real-world robot manipulation. On adapted VirtualHome and RoboCasa benchmarks, CausGround improves vision-based high-level planning across MLLM backbones, increasing average task success from 24.9% to 53.3% on VirtualHome and closed-loop planning success from 43.0% to 57.0% on RoboCasa. On SWITCH, average interaction reasoning accuracy increases from 25.8% to 31.9%, indicating improved action selection, recovery, and verification. Moreover, real-world experiments show that CausGround reasons backward from MLLM-interpreted goals to derive executable high-level action sequences for sequential manipulation. These results demonstrate that reusable action preconditions and effects support long-horizon embodied planning across diverse environments without parameter updates.

## 10. [HuGe: Scaling Humanoid Generalist Vision-Language-Action Models with Egocentric Experience](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34186)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.34186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34186)

**Authors**: Yang Liu, Jiacheng Liu, Hengtao Li, Yuheng Huang, Zijian Yang, Ji Dai, Maoxin Wang, Yi Gao, Qishen Wang, Baohua Yin, Peiyang Li, Shuanghao Bai, Mingyang Sun, Pengxiang Ding, Jianqin Yin, Lixin Yang, Donglin Wang

**Published**: 2026-10-25 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Yang Liu

**Upvotes**: 1

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/yliu-cs/HuGe) | [Project Page](https://yliu-cs.github.io/HuGe)

### Abstract

Extending Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) to humanoid robots requires coordinated whole-body control and diverse demonstrations that are costly to collect in real-world scenarios. Although egocentric human demonstrations provide a complementary source of task experience, differences in visual embodiment and missing robot proprioception complicate transfer to humanoid policies. To address these challenges, we introduce HuGe, a family of Humanoid Generalist VLAs pre-trained on over 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data. Specifically, HuGe employs three existing representative VLA architectures to predict latent motion tokens for a pretrained whole-body controller, together with hand-control signals. Based on this, we introduce a three-stage training pipeline that combines egocentric pre-training, humanoid adaptation, and downstream fine-tuning. To facilitate transfer across embodiments, we construct pseudo-proprioceptive states through simulation playback during pre-training and modify arm appearance in egocentric observations through re-rendering during downstream fine-tuning. With this pipeline, HuGe achieves the state-of-the-art on simulation benchmark HumanoidArena. Furthermore, real-world experiments on the Unitree G1 indicate that augmenting robot demonstrations with visually adapted egocentric demonstrations significantly increases success on a pick-and-drop task.

## 11. [OmniSpectra: A Unified Foundation Model for Universal Spectra Representation Learning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.15351)

**ArXiv ID**: [2601.15351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15351)

**Authors**: Md Khairul Islam, Zeyu Xia, Adam S. Bolton, Judy Fox

**Published**: 2026-10-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-10-29 by Zeyu Xia

**Upvotes**: 0

### Abstract

Standard sequence models encode position by token rank rather than physical coordinate, typically forcing practitioners to destructively resample continuous physical signals such as spectra observed across heterogeneous instruments. We identify this coordinate mismatch as a structural limitation in cross-instrument representation learning, and propose OmniSpectra, a wavelength-native foundation model for spectral representation learning: a continuous-coordinate masked autoencoder that discards the integer pixel indexing of sequence modeling. Mapping physical coordinates to a logarithmic basis turns the multiplicative scaling induced by cosmological redshift into a uniform additive shift. In continuous rotary position embeddings, this shared shift cancels from relative token distances rather than producing grid-dependent positional distortion. We further counter the spectral bias inherent to implicit neural decoders with a physics-grounded, resolution-gated Fourier decoder that provides anti-aliasing. Pretrained on eight surveys and benchmarked against seven baselines, our model reaches state-of-the-art performance with only 12.2M parameters while outperforming substantially larger models, indicating that encoding physical structure can substitute for parameter scale.
