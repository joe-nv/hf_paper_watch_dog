## 1. [Motion Mamba 2.0: Autoregressive Diffusion Mamba for Long-Horizon Humanoid Control](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29897)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.29897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29897)

**Organization**: Peking University

**Authors**: Yue Huang, Tianshan Zhang, Zeyu Zhang, Jayeon Kang, Akide Liu, Sungjoon Choi, Dacheng Tao, Hao Tang

**Published**: 2026-11-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Zeyu Zhang

**Upvotes**: 171

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/AIGeeksGroup/MotionMamba2.0) | [Project Page](https://aigeeksgroup.github.io/MotionMamba2.0/)

### Abstract

Long-horizon, real-time controllable motion generation is essential for interactive animation and humanoid robotics, where an agent must respond to changing instructions and spatial goals while moving. Existing autoregressive diffusion methods generate motion incrementally, but bounded attention caches limit access to distant history, while repeated denoising introduces substantial inference overhead. We present Motion Mamba 2.0, a Mamba-based root-body diffusion architecture that integrates local attention, resumable recurrent states, and persistent content memory (PCM) for long-horizon controllable motion generation. PCM transforms compressed motion history from Mamba recurrent states, together with state changes and text-conditioned features, into dynamically updated content slots accessible to local attention. To enable efficient inference, we further develop an execution scheme tailored to this architecture, combining CUDA Graph replay, fused kernels, and execution-flow simplification while preserving the canonical history update. Evaluations on retargeted HiPHI demonstrate accurate terminal control over rollouts of up to 39.52 s, while short-horizon comparisons on BONES Seed and retargeted HumanML3D show that the model also retains strong spatial control and text-motion matching. On an RTX 4090, the optimized execution reduces median complete-window latency from 1685.35 to 276.14 ms, achieving a median paired speedup of 6.138times while preserving identical outputs and committed states in the tested rollouts. Moreover, we conduct extensive real-world evaluation on the Unitree G1, demonstrating sequential text-guided action composition, expressive motion, and interactive locomotion through keyboard and mouse control.

## 2. [Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31201)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.31201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31201)

**Organization**: MMLab@NTU

**Authors**: Xiao-Ming Wu, Kang Liao, Yuexiao Ma, Taolin Zhang, Mingqian Zhou, Nuo Chen, Ziming Li, Yihang Luo, Rui Li, Zhonghua Wu, Tianyu Xie, Xiawu Zheng, Zhongang Cai, Zhi Hou, Chen Change Loy

**Published**: 2026-11-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by wu xiaoming

**Upvotes**: 89

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/DravenALG/Embo-Harness) | [Project Page](https://dravenalg.github.io/projects/Embo-Harness/)

### Abstract

Frontier multimodal models exhibit extraordinary generalization in generating robot actions from observations and instructions, enabling a new paradigm in which they serve directly as policies within closed-loop robotic harnesses. However, the design principles of such harnesses, and the capabilities underlying their strong performance, remain unclear. We present Embo-Harness, a framework for systematically studying frontier-model-as-policy harnesses. We investigate four components of the robotic agentic loop, namely, prompting, planning, action execution, and tool use, and distill six empirical findings into a practical recipe. This recipe prioritizes interfaces that exploit the model's native visual reasoning and tool-use capabilities over handcrafted plans, skills, or action priors, and identifies effective choices for action representation, chunking, reasoning effort, visual observations, short multi-action sequences, and policy refinement. On RoboLab-120, Embo-Harness achieves an 83.3\% success rate, outperforming the strongest reported baseline by 26.2 percentage points. It also succeeds in 15 of 20 zero-shot trials across four real-world manipulation tasks. Beyond performance, controlled interventions indicate that the studied model depends more strongly on accurate spatial target estimation than on explicit low-level joint or trajectory generation. These results provide both a practical recipe for building strong frontier-model-as-policy harnesses and a clearer account of the capabilities that drive their performance.

## 3. [WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30989)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.30989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30989)

**Organization**: National University of Singapore

**Authors**: Xinyang Zhou, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang

**Published**: 2026-11-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Qi Li

**Upvotes**: 69

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/xyangzhou/WorldLeague) | [Project Page](https://xyangzhou.github.io/WorldLeague/)

### Abstract

AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce WorldLeague, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of 60 simulated robot tasks by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in WorldSeek, rearrange objects in WorldShape, and adapt to changes in WorldShift. Tying these goals to a single control interface limits which AI systems can be compared. In WorldLeague, vision-language models (VLMs) choose targets and motions, while vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs) use their native controls. For each of six capability dimensions, we measure how much progress a system makes toward its goals (Interactive Score) and how often it achieves them (physical success rate). Together, these measures form the system's Physical Agency Profile. Among 13 representative AI systems, Claude Fable 5.2 achieves 13 of the 60 goals and earns the highest mean Interactive Score (28.58/100). Fable leads in four dimensions, while GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tie for first on rule-following tasks. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8\% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion. Project Webpage: https://xyangzhou.github.io/WorldLeague

### Abstract

Animating arbitrary 3D assets from instructions requires both structural inference and motion control, yet existing motion benchmarks mostly cover conventional human or animal skeletons with simple instructions. We introduce DivDynBench, which evaluates rigging and motion generation across six morphology categories, with instructions requiring multi-phase part coordination and explicit timing, angle, repetition, and trajectory constraints. ## 6. [Let the Fixer Decide: Training a Code Localizer from the Fixer's Likelihood](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12164)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.12164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12164)

**Organization**: Misraj Ai

**Authors**: Khalil Hennara, Muhammad Hreden, Zeina Aldallal, Sara Chrouf, Safwan AlModhayan

**Published**: 2026-11-09 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Khalil Hennara

**Upvotes**: 51

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/misraj-ai/locfix)

### Abstract

Code localizers are usually trained to predict the files a reference patch edits, but the code a fixer needs to read is not the same as the code it changes. We propose Fixer-Guided Localization (FGL): the localizer proposes several localizations, a frozen fixer scores each by how likely it makes a verified patch, and the localizer is trained on these scores with weighted supervised fine-tuning, without running tests and without reinforcement learning. We build the full pipeline on Gemma 4 E4B for the Kaggle Gemma-4 Developer Agent competition, from 1,629 test-verified Kimi K3 trajectories. The FGL target helps the fixer find the bug as often as the teacher's localization while using 27.5% less code. On the development set, one EM round moves the agent in the expected direction compared with a matched control: more tasks reach a patch (53 vs. 46), fewer tasks get stuck reading files that do not exist (43 vs. 54), and more tasks are solved (8 vs. 6). Training also changes how the agent works: trained agents explore about three times more than the base model and write far fewer wrong patches, and the single agent solves up to 11 of 78 tasks, against 8 for the base model. An analysis of 13 evaluation runs identifies what limits a 4B agent under an 8-minute budget (time, repetition loops and edit quality) and gives practical lessons for training small coding agents. All data, code and adapters are publicly available.

## 7. [A Glimpse of Training VLMs as Foundation Models for Robotic Manipulation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29338)

### Abstract

Recently, GPT-6 Astra demonstrates strong generalization in robot control, yet limited disclosure of its training leaves open how such capabilities can be reproduced. We introduce nanoAstra, an approach to training vision-language models (VLMs) for generalizable manipulation that combines semantic action modeling with capability-preserving supervised fine-tuning (CP-SFT). nanoAstra represents robot behavior as semantic steps composed of reusable, parameterized action primitives, guided by explicit progress descriptions. ## 8. [ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26389)

### Abstract

Current leading video generation models can synthesize highly realistic videos, while still frequently violating physical and logical constraints in multi-step reasoning and planning, even after training with large-scale reasoning data or existing diffusion RL methods. We attribute these failures to the conventional video training paradigm, which treats a solution as a fixed-length clip rather than a chain of decisions. We present ViDecide, a decision-centric framework that trains video diffusion models to predict state transitions causally and decide when to stop, with a decision-focused training schedule. ## 9. [AutoPILOT: A Visual Agent Harness for Real-Time Gameplay](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28202)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.28202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28202)

**Organization**: University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign

**Authors**: Mark Zhao, Pengcheng Wang, Rui Yang, Jiarui Yao, Xirui Jiang, Yuxi Chen, Bingxuan Li, Hanyang Chen, Ke Yang, Manling Li, Tong Zhang, Huan Zhang

**Published**: 2026-11-20 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Rui Yang

**Upvotes**: 32

**Links**: [Project Page](https://autopilot-harness.github.io/)

### Abstract

Multimodal foundation models can play video games well when the game waits for them, but often fail when it keeps running. We find that much of this gap lies in the harness, which decides what the model sees, what it remembers, and how its outputs become actions over time. Existing harnesses such as VISTA improve the first two, yet turn each model call into at most one action, so the agent acts only as often as its model thinks. We introduce AutoPILOT, which changes the third: in each model call, the model can send a primitive action, start an open-loop macro, or write a closed-loop controller that keeps playing from the screen without further model calls until it hands control back. To evaluate agents in real-time games, we build AFK-Bench, 120 diverse browser games grouped by how they respond to inaction and playable in real time or with the clock paused. With GPT-6.1 Sol under the same budgets, AutoPILOT reaches 68.6% real-time success against 38.3% for VISTA, and on GameWorld it keeps 80.0% in real time while VISTA drops from 84.1% to 42.9%. Its gains hold across models, and it reaches VISTA's final success rate at a fifth of its API cost. Our results suggest that how a harness turns model outputs into actions over time is a central yet underexplored part of agent design, and we hope AutoPILOT and AFK-Bench will serve as a foundation for agents in a world that does not wait.

## 11. [World Models as Native Latent Action Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30898)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.30898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30898)

**Organization**: SenseTime

**Authors**: Qinyue Li, Weijie Qiao, Qingyi Tao, Zerui Gong, Zhe Yang, Quanzhou Li, Taolin Zhang, Jinglin He, Zhonghua Wu, Chen Change Loy

**Published**: 2026-11-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Tao Qingyi

**Upvotes**: 23

**Links**: [Project Page](https://pawteam-eai.github.io/native-lam/)

### Abstract

Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.

## 12. [Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation of RL Experts: Toward Continual Post-Training](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27376)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.27376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27376)

**Organization**: UCSB AI Group

**Authors**: Zhen Zhang, Changyi Yang, Xuehai He, Chengzhi Liu, Yutong Yin, Ziyue Liu, Zhuoran Yin, Yangmin Li, Zhihan Liu, Jisen Li, Mingyu Jin, Chuhan Li, Zhaotian Weng, Ruoyu He, Wenyao Gao, Jiaxin Deng, Chenwei Wu, Hui Shen, Wenhao Chai, Chenyang Zhao, Zhuofan Ying, Kieran Liu, Andrew Chen, Pony Ma, Zhaoran Wang, Alkesh Patel, Zhe Gan, Xin Eric Wang

**Upvotes**: 21

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/UCSB-AI/CSPD) | [Project Page](https://github.com/UCSB-AI/CSPD)

### Abstract

RL post-training typically produces domain experts that must then be consolidated into one model, and as new experts keep arriving, this consolidation becomes a continual process. Weight merging and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) must re-consolidate the whole model whenever an expert is added, and can erode capabilities outside the target domains. We propose Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation (CSPD), which keeps the pre-RL model frozen, distills each RL expert independently into a soft prompt, and composes experts by concatenating their prompts without joint training. A single soft prompt suffices to distill an RL expert, recovering at least 90% of the teacher's gain in 22 of 23 math, code, and agent settings. Independently trained prompts compose well. Input-space adaptation adds little serving overhead, keeping throughput within 1.5% of the unadapted backbone and reaching 2.40x that of rank-4 LoRA. These results point toward continual post-training on a fixed backbone.

## 14. [ViDiHand2: Unifying Video Diffusion Priors and Hand Geometry for 3D Hand Motion Reconstruction in the Wild](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30597)

### Abstract

Learning robot manipulation from egocentric human videos requires accurate metric 3D hand motion. The gain reverses when each episode is weighted equally and vanishes when only episodes on the pruned tasks are balanced. Without pruning, length weighting there reinforces long detour successes that erode skills on other tasks. Following these findings, we build MobileGym-Lite, which keeps reward and data coverage complete and prunes unneeded features. It outperforms Full in the full environment (55.48% versus 51.61% success) with lower construction cost.

## 49. [Self-Supervised Dataset Distillation via Mutual Information Maximization](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31225)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.31225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31225)

**Authors**: Mohammad Hossein Moslemi, Mahdi Beitollahi, Zhimin Mei, Jiale Cai, Bissan Ghaddar, Boyu Wang

**Published**: 2026-11-23 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Mohammad Hossein Moslemi

**Upvotes**: 2

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/mhmoslemi/MIPA)

### Abstract

Dataset distillation (DD) aims to compress a large dataset into a small synthetic one that remains effective for training neural networks. Despite its success in supervised learning, DD remains underexplored in self-supervised learning (SSL), where distilled data must preserve the information needed to learn representations that remain consistent across augmentations. To bridge this gap, we introduce Mutual Information Prototype Alignment (MIPA), an efficient SSL dataset distillation approach that optimizes synthetic images to preserve augmentation-invariant information. Specifically, MIPA treats synthetic images as learnable prototypes and uses a frozen encoder to assign real samples to them based on feature similarity. A synthetic dataset that preserves such augmentation invariance should yield stable prototype assignments across multiple augmented views of the same real image. MIPA encourages this by maximizing mutual information between cross-view assignments while avoiding trivial solutions in which all real samples are assigned to only a few synthetic prototypes. Evaluations on image classification benchmarks show that synthetic data generated by MIPA yield higher-quality representations than existing distillation approaches while reducing time and storage demands during distillation.

## 50. [Measuring and Teaching Language Model Agents to Act on Delayed Consequences](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28158)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.28158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28158)

**Organization**: ORO AI

**Authors**: Shardul Bansal, Seth Schilbe, Jarrod Barnes

**Published**: 2026-11-19 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-24 by Seth Schilbe

**Upvotes**: 2

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/ORO-AI/smb-bench) | [Project Page](https://oroagents.com/benchmarks/smb-bench)

### Abstract

Language model agents are increasingly asked to run ongoing multi-day operations rather than finish isolated single-turn tasks. In daily operations, today's choices reshape later observations and cash constraints because empty shelves hide lost walk-in demand, short-shipped customers quietly shrink future orders weeks later, and emergency buying drains working capital. We introduce SMB Bench, a calibrated 90-day wholesale simulator with 18 causally paired tasks and an 859-run frontier trace dataset, to ask what experience and learning signal make an agent better at noticing, attributing, and acting on the delayed consequences of its own decisions in unseen businesses. Across 16 frontier models, only 3 outperform a fixed buying rule; models often record a relevant diagnosis in their notes without changing later purchases in time, showing that diagnosing a problem, changing behavior, and improving a business are separate achievements. To study how experience improves those failures in an open model (Qwen3.8-27B), we used training-business records to identify operational rules and archived frontier quarters as starting states to continue from rather than transcripts to copy. On an unseen test business under identical evaluation instructions, the trained policy followed customer-protection commitments more consistently and reduced severe-loss quarters, while the additional profit benefit remained uncertain. Carrying those commitments through the quarter exposed the cost of fulfilling them, as early-quarter stocking habits left the buyer relying on emergency supply and a deteriorating supplier. The results separate recognizing a delayed effect, acting on it consistently, and coordinating surrounding decisions so that the response improves profit. SMB Bench connects these observations to a learning method by turning archived decisions and their consequences into reusable training experience.
