## 1. [Wave Forcing: Towards Speed-of-Light Streaming Video Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00859)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.00859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00859)

**Organization**: DENG Lab @ SJTU

**Authors**: Yijie Jin, Yuxuan Liu, Jiajun Xu, Ye Huang, Chuzhi Wang, Jiayang Li, Ruiyang Chen, Chenling Hong, Xu Wang, Zhijie Deng

**Published**: 2026-10-31 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-03 by Yijie Jin

**Upvotes**: 96

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/SJTU-DENG-Lab/WaveForcing) | [Project Page](https://sjtu-deng-lab.github.io/WaveForcing/)

### Abstract

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Existing chunk-wise autoregressive methods face challenges in both generation efficiency and long-video quality: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, and longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. We propose Wave Forcing (WF), which co-designs generation, training, and execution for continuous video output with multi-step denoising. WF uses chunk-wise causal attention to access earlier, cleaner chunks before their denoising is complete, and distributes staggered denoising and cache-refresh stages across full-model replicas to achieve 1F1C: one output chunk per concurrent forward round after pipeline fill. To adapt the model to this continuous generation process, we develop a three-stage training procedure that successively introduces mixed-noise denoising, motion supervision, and causal adaptation using self-generated history. To execute this process efficiently, Wave Runtime (WaveRT) uses Wave Parallelism (WP) for early K/V delivery and communication-computation overlap, together with optimized kernels and pipelined VAE decoding. Experiments on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B show that 5-step WF maintains overall quality comparable to Rolling Forcing while achieving higher Dynamic Degree on both short and long videos. Our system efficiency experiments show that 4-step WF achieves 147.6 DiT FPS and 134.8 end-to-end FPS on eight H200 GPUs, yielding 7.07x the single-GPU end-to-end throughput. In DiT-only tests with matched SageAttention+W8A8 backends, denoising steps, and GPU counts, WP delivers up to 2.53x the throughput of the faster of the Ulysses and Ring context-parallel baselines across six model scales (1.3B-14B).

### Abstract

Despite recent advances in CUAs, their development remains constrained by the limited diversity, controllability, and reliability of interaction data. Moreover, conventional optimization pipelines lack iterative feedback, while existing recursive improvement approaches often rely on self-generated experiences, limiting ability expansion and yielding diminishing returns. To address these challenges, we introduce CUA-Master, a nested double-loop recursive improvement framework. At the system level, an outer world-data-model flywheel continuously incorporates open-world corpus and leverages evolving model weaknesses to guide subsequent data construction and optimization iterations. At the data level, an inner flywheel couples open-world seeding and autocatalytic propagation. ## 8. [Lost in Recovery: Demystifying Recovery Behavior of Computer-Use Agents via Goal-Oriented Action Replay](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27954)

**ArXiv ID**: [2610.27954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27954)

**Organization**: KAIST AI

**Authors**: Byeonghun Bang, Sangwoo Park, Jinheon Baek, Kangsan Kim, Youngwan Lee, Sung Ju Hwang

**Published**: 2026-10-20 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-03 by Byeonghun Bang

**Upvotes**: 27

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/BangProx/ReTRACE)

### Abstract

Recent advances in large (vision) language models have enabled computer-use agents that automate digital workflows via fine-grained keyboard and mouse operations. However, such agents remain fragile in long-horizon settings, where errors accumulate and executed actions leave unintended side effects, making the ability to recover from failures critical. Yet, existing remedies remain inadequate: environment resets are infeasible in proprietary settings, action replay is prohibitively expensive over long horizons, and API-based rollback is limited to web environments, leaving model-based recovery largely unexplored. To address this gap, we present ReTrace, a scalable evaluation framework for systematically studying recovery behavior in computer-use agents. We formalize Transition-Consistent Recovery: identical downstream actions from the recovered and target states must yield identical outcomes. We operationalize this criterion through goal-oriented action replay following controlled error injection. Using ReTrace, we present the first systematic study of autonomous, model-based recovery, revealing that (i) ideal recovery can unlock model's latent task completion capabilities, (ii) current computer-use agents fail to exploit their latent recovery capabilities, and (iii) accurate model-based recovery yields measurable end-to-end gains. Together, these findings establish recovery as a fundamental capability for building reliable computer-use agents. Our code and dataset is available at https://github.com/BangProx/ReTRACE.

## 9. [SWE-Trail: History-Guided Trajectory Collection for Coding Agent Training](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02074)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.02074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02074)

**Organization**: Ant Group

**Authors**: Ziming Li, Lu Yu, Jiatan Huang, Qing Cui, Jun Zhou, Chuxu Zhang

**Published**: 2026-11-01 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-03 by ziming li

**Upvotes**: 26

### Abstract

Coding agents solve repository issues through long sequences of exploration, editing, and validation. These trajectories are increasingly used as post-training data, so how they are collected shapes what the trained model learns. A common approach is to sample multiple trajectories independently for each issue and then select or curate them. However, as more trajectories are sampled independently, later trajectories become increasingly redundant, even though earlier trajectories could guide where subsequent collection effort should be spent. A natural way to use this history is to expose it to the coding agent as advice. But training on such trajectories can make the model rely on advice that is unavailable at test time, creating a train-test mismatch. Instead, we argue that history should guide where collection effort is spent, while the coding agent decides what to generate. We introduce SWE-Trail, a trajectory collector that uses earlier trajectories to guide subsequent collection while keeping that history outside the coding agent's context. During a new rollout, SWE-Trail uses history to identify a state where additional collection effort may be useful. There, the coding agent proposes candidate next actions from its ordinary context, and SWE-Trail briefly executes them from the same checkpoint to decide which route continues. We evaluate collection approaches by training student models on the trajectories they produce. For a 124B student initialized from Ling-3.0-flash-base, SWE-Trail improves mean resolution over independent sampling by 2.33 and 1.60 percentage points on SWE-bench Multilingual and SWE-bench Pro, respectively. Without history, the same pipeline produces more resolved trajectories than independent sampling, but improves by only 0.11 points on Multilingual. These results show the value of using history to guide collection without exposing it to the coding agent.

## 11. [Reviso: Not All Visual Evidence Deserves Equal Attention in Video Captioning](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03602)

**Authors**: Tengfei Liu, Yang Shi, Yuran Wang, Yuqi Tang, Yizhuo Jia, Zhuoran Zhang, Qixun Wang, Shujie Li, Xuanyu Zhu, Xinyu Liu, Minghui Guo, Shixuan Liu, Haotian Wang, Yuanxing Zhang, Pengfei Wan

**Published**: 2026-11-02 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-03 by Zhu

**Upvotes**: 24

### Abstract

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can generate detailed video captions, yet they may overemphasize minor details while overlooking essential context and key events. We argue that not all visual evidence deserves equal attention, as the importance of visual information depends on the content of each video. Motivated by this observation, we propose Reviso, an agentic video captioning framework that learns what to observe and how to gather relevant visual evidence. From a low-frame-rate global preview, Reviso constructs a hierarchical Context--Focus--Points (CFP) observation plan that identifies the video context, prioritizes key aspects, and defines concrete observation targets and corresponding tools. Guided by this plan, Reviso performs goal-directed active perception by selectively revisiting temporal segments and combining tools for fine-grained inspection. It interprets the acquired evidence, updates its plan as needed, and integrates global and local information to generate the final caption. To learn this observation strategy, we develop a verification-guided pipeline that constructs high-quality training trajectories through factual verification and teacher-guided refinement. These findings highlight the effectiveness and potential of priority-guided active observation for video captioning.

## 14. [When Does Latent Prediction Error Support Physical Surprise Claims in Video World Models?](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01815)

**ArXiv ID**: [2611.01815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01815)

**Authors**: Anton Hristov, Aleksandar Slavov, Vasil Vasilev

**Published**: 2026-11-01 | **Submitted**: 2026-11-03 by Vasil Vasilev

**Upvotes**: 16

**Links**: [GitHub](https://github.com/3-ml/jepa-surprise) | [Project Page](https://3-ml.github.io/jepa-surprise/)

### Abstract

Prediction errors from video world models are often treated as physical surprise, but they can also reflect appearance, static content, motion, or ordinary prediction difficulty. We present an evaluation framework that separates what frozen representations contain, how they organize appearance and physical differences, and what changes a model's own prediction error. On IntPhys2, a benchmark of physically possible and impossible events, we compare representations from latent prediction, masked pixel reconstruction, and simple visual statistics. Appearance remains easy to decode, while V-JEPA2 exposes modest information about whether events are possible or impossible. Looking at the latent geometry, appearance changes become smaller compared with differences between videos, their directions line up more consistently across scenes, and their overlap with physical changes decreases in deeper layers. Across action-conditioned models, incorrect actions raise prediction error, while appearance changes also produce substantial responses. In Physion++ video clips, repeating a single frame can produce higher error than the original clip, while changing color throughout the clip can lower it. Using Kubric, a simulation and rendering framework, we find that impossible events can produce lower error than possible events. A fixed error threshold also incorrectly flags many possible events as impossible. A model can therefore represent physical information and respond to incorrect actions without its native prediction error reliably measuring physical surprise.
